What actually moves the price

Four levers explain almost every gap between two quotes for the same house.

Distance and truck size

Transport is billed on the truck, not your stuff: a 17-ft truck costs roughly double a Tata Ace per km. An oversized truck "for safety" is a classic padding line.

Floors without a lift

Stair carrying for heavy items typically adds ₹500–₹2,000 per floor. It applies at both ends, and again if the truck can't park near the entrance.

Packing grade

Multi-layer packing (foam + bubble + corrugated + lamination) can add 50–60% to the packing line. Worth it for TVs and glass; waste on clothes and books.

Timing and truck-entry windows

Month-end weekends are peak season in Bangalore — every lease in the city seems to end on the 30th. BBMP truck-entry restrictions can also stretch a move by 1–2 hours.

Before you pay a token advance

Most moving-day horror stories from Bangalore start with one of these being skipped.

  • Get an itemised quote in writing. Packing, labour, transport and taxes as separate lines. A single lump sum hides where the padding is.
  • Cap the advance at 10–20%. Balance after unloading. A mover demanding 50%+ upfront before the truck arrives is a risk, not a discount.
  • Insist on a GST invoice. 18% GST is mandatory on a legitimate bill. "No bill, lower price" also means no proof if your goods are damaged or held.
  • Sign an inventory list at loading. Every item and its condition, countersigned by the crew lead. It is your only leverage in a damage claim.
  • Book the lift with your society. Many Bangalore apartments need a prior booking and a refundable deposit for moving day; some ban Sunday moves. Check before fixing the date.
  • Confirm dismantling in the quote. Beds, wardrobes and wall units: who dismantles, who reassembles, and is it priced in? "We'll see on the day" means an extra charge on the day.
